Voice: a calm, warm mathematics lecturer, explaining to a curious, intelligent audience who are hearing this for the first time.

Pace: slow and deliberate, about 125 words per minute, much slower than everyday speech. Keep this slow pace from the first sentence to the very last; do not speed up in later paragraphs. Never rush, and read every sentence to its end.

Pauses: pause naturally at the end of every sentence, as a lecturer does while the audience takes in the board, and briefly at commas. The text is divided into paragraphs by blank lines. Between paragraphs, take a longer, natural breath before the new idea, the way a speaker turns to a new part of the board.

Tone: conversational and engaged, not an announcer. Let the pitch rise and fall naturally through each sentence, and carry the thread from one sentence to the next.

Lists: give each item its own small falling tone, with a pause before the next.

Symbols: single letters such as d, H, u, n, V, a, b, z and E, and Greek letters such as lambda, eta, kappa and delta, are mathematical symbols; say each one clearly and a little more slowly, as a lecturer would at the board.

Pronunciation: Erdős is AIR-dush; Bethe is BAY-tuh; Salmhofer is SAHLM-hoh-fer; Mathlib is MATH-lib; nanoda is NAH-noh-dah.
